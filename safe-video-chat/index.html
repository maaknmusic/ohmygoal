How to stay safe on video chat

Most video chats are fun and harmless. A few people use them to scam or blackmail. This guide explains how those scams start, how to avoid them, and exactly what to do if it happens to you.

How video call blackmail (sextortion) usually starts

  1. Someone friendly and attractive starts chatting, often very quickly flattering you.
  2. They ask you to move to another app (WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram) or to show more on camera.
  3. They record the call. Sometimes the “person” is a pre-recorded video.
  4. They threaten to send the recording to your family, friends or employer unless you pay, usually by UPI.

If someone is blackmailing you

  1. Do not pay. Paying almost never makes them delete anything. It tells them you will pay again.
  2. Stop replying. Do not negotiate or argue.
  3. Keep evidence: screenshots of messages, usernames, the UPI ID or number they gave.
  4. Report: call 1930 or file at cybercrime.gov.in. Report and block the account on every app they used.
  5. Tell someone you trust. You are the victim of a crime, not the one at fault.
  6. If you are under 18 or the images involve someone under 18, Take It Down can help get images removed.

Other scams to watch for

  • UPI and “investment” scams: a new “friend” suggests a trading app or asks you to send money to unlock a payment. Never send money to someone you met in a chat.
  • Fake police or officials: real police do not call to demand money over a video call.
  • Links: do not open links from strangers or install apps they suggest.

Questions

What is sextortion?

Sextortion is blackmail using intimate images or video, often recorded during a video call. The criminal threatens to share it unless you pay or send more.

Should I pay if someone threatens to share my video?

No. Do not pay and stop replying. Keep evidence and report it: in India call 1930 or use cybercrime.gov.in.

Can OhMyGoal see or record my calls?

No. Video and messages go directly between the two people and are not stored by us. That is also why you should be careful: the other person can still record their own screen.

Where do I report in India?

Call 1930, the National Cyber Crime Helpline, or report online at cybercrime.gov.in. In an emergency call 112.
